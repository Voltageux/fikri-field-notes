## Where the help fits

I'm using AI assistance as part of a broader effort to identify appropriate work and organize application information better. That brings up a simple question: which parts can a tool help with, and which parts still need my review?

An automation tool can summarize a job posting adn draft responses. Those are useful places for assistance to fit. But a draft response can include claims about the person applying, so checking it means more than reading whether the sentences sound okay.

This is an explanatory note about safe workflow design. It isn't a report of applications being automatically submitted today. The point here is the place human review needs to have in that workflow.

## What still needs checking

Claims abuot degrees, work history, and availability need to be verified by the applicant. For my own application information, that means the check still belongs to me, even when a tool helps put the words together.

I think that distinction is worth keeping clear. Summarizing a posting is one kind of help. Drafting an answer about my background brings in information that I need to verify. Having a draft ready doesn't remove that step.

So the review needs to ask something quite direct: are the claims about degrees, work history, and availability correct? That's the check I mean here. AI assistance can be part of organizing the information, while human review remains part of deciding whether the drafted response is accurate.
