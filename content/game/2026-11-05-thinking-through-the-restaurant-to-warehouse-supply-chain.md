## Where the warehouse fits

Crust & Crime is an unfinished game development project exploring multiple restaurants, ingredient inventory, and city logistics. This note is abuot part of that design: how warehouse storage and deliveries might fit together.

I want to reason through those areas without treating them as finished mechanics. There is a difference between explaining how a supply chain could work and saying that it already works in the game. For now, this is a plan for thinking through the design.

The main question is how ingredients should move between suppliers, warehouse inventory, and restaurant inventory. Should those movements follow the same schedule, or should each part have its own timing? That seems like a useful place to start, before treating the whole route as one delivery process.

## What different schedules might mean

One possible future comparison wuold be to consider a shared schedule, then consider separate schedules for ingredients arriving at the warehouse and ingredients leaving for restaurants. The point would be to ask what each arrangement means for inventory at each step.

If the schedules were separate, what would need to be considered at the warehouse? And how would that relate to ingredient inventory across multiple restaurants? These are questions to work through, rather than findings from a completed test.

For this planning note, I want to keep the focus there: warehouse storage, deliveries, and whether their timing should be considered separately.
