## What changes with the hydrograph?

How might a different inflow hydrograph change the assumptions I supply to a numerical model? That's the question I want to keep at the centre of a future unsteady hydrodynamic simulation. I haven't run this study in HEC-RAS; this is a plan for what I might learn from boundary-condition sensitivity.

The idea starts with the hydrograph's shape, timing, and peak. I'd like to consider each of those when thinking about the inflow boundary condition. What if the shape were different? What if the timing changed, or the peak? These are questions to explore, wiht no particular outcome assumed yet.

I think keeping the question this simple would help me describe what I'm asking of the hypothetical model. Before talking about possible results, I want to be clear about which inflow assumptions those results would depend on.

## Keeping two questions separate

The other part is how I wuold describe any future results. Uncertainty in the input and the performance of the model are separate questions, and I want my wording to keep that distinction clear.

If I considered a different hydrograph, I'd need to explain the change in the supplied assumptions before making any claim about model performance. A question about sensitivity shouldn't quietly turn into a judgment about how well the model works.

I'm leaving specific discharge and water surface values out of this plan. The next check would be to make the input question clear: what am I changing about the hydrograph, and what would I be trying to learn from that change?
